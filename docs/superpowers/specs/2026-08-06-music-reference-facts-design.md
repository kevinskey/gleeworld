# Music Reference Facts for the GleeWorld Assistant

Date: 2026-08-06
Status: Proposed — awaiting review. Supersedes the BLOCKED
`2026-08-06-music-reference-library-design.md`.
Branch: `feat/music-reference-library`

## Why this replaces the previous design

The earlier spec ingested ~70,000 words of prose from three reference documents
derived from published sources. That was blocked: this repository already ships
`_shared/liturgy/corpus.ts` **empty** rather than bundle copyrighted text, and
the corpus deploys commercially to roughly 50 client organizations.

This design keeps the part that was blocked for the wrong reason. **Facts are
not copyrightable; expression is.** A violin's range is a physical property of
the instrument. B♭ clarinet sounding a major second below written pitch is
arithmetic. *Kind of Blue* was recorded in 1959. None of that becomes anyone's
property by being printed in a textbook. The paragraphs explaining why the G
string is "dark, throaty, powerful" are expression, and they stay out.

## The core idea

**The corpus stores structured data, and its prose is generated by our own
template code.** No sentence from any source document reaches `corpus.ts`.

```
docs/reference/data/*.json     hand-curated FACTS (committed, reviewable)
        │
        │  npm run ingest:musicfacts
        │  renders each record through OUR templates
        ▼
_shared/musicfacts/corpus.ts   generated chunks, our wording
```

A record like:

```json
{
  "instrument": "Viola", "family": "strings",
  "transposition": "none", "clefs": ["alto", "treble"],
  "range": { "full": ["C3", "A6"], "section": ["C3", "D6"] }
}
```

renders to a chunk whose text is written by the template, e.g.:

> **Viola — range and transposition.** Non-transposing; sounds as written.
> Notated in alto clef, switching to treble for sustained high passages. Full
> range C3–A6. Practical section range C3–D6.

The facts are the document's; the sentence is ours.

## What gets extracted

| Domain | Records | Fields |
|---|---|---|
| Instruments | ~45 | name, family, transposition, clefs, full range, practical ranges, register spans |
| Score order | ~8 ensembles | ensemble, ordered instrument list |
| Ensemble rosters | ~10 | ensemble type, standard instrumentation counts |
| Voice ranges | ~8 | voice part, full range, practical range, passaggio pitches |
| Jazz chronology | ~60 | year, event stated in our own words |
| Recording canon | ~100 | artist, album, year, label |

## What is deliberately dropped

- Every prose paragraph: character, idiomatic writing, cautions, history,
  analysis, technique discussion.
- The "Character" column of every register table — that column is the
  interesting part of those tables and it is expression.
- Glossary and answer-card entries. A definition is a written explanation, not
  a data point.

**Be honest about the cost.** This is a much smaller and duller corpus than the
original design: hundreds of records instead of ~500 prose chunks, and it
answers "what is the viola's range" but not "how should I score for violas."
The judgment is that the second question is one the base model already handles
acceptably, while the first is exactly where it invents plausible numbers.

## Open issue: practical ranges are judgment, not physics

A violin's full range G3–C8 is a physical fact. A "practical section range of
G3–B6" is the author's editorial judgment about what a real section can play —
closer to expression, and thinner ice.

Recommendation: ingest **full ranges** as facts, and treat **practical ranges**
as GleeWorld's own guidance — reviewed by Kevin against his own experience
before they ship, and worded as our recommendation rather than reproduced.
Where he does not want to vouch for a number, omit it; a chunk with a full
range and no practical range is still useful.

This needs Kevin's decision before ingest, since it changes what he is putting
his name to.

## Architecture

Follows the `_shared/liturgy/` precedent exactly, which the previous spec got
wrong. `_shared/academy/search.ts` on main is **already generic**
(`buildIndex<C extends SearchableChunk>`), and `_shared/academy/types.ts`
already exports `SearchableChunk`, `Hit<C>` and `KnowledgeIndex<C>` with a
comment anticipating reuse. So:

- `_shared/musicfacts/types.ts` — `MusicFactChunk extends SearchableChunk`, adding `id`, `domain`, `subject`.
- `_shared/musicfacts/corpus.ts` — generated.
- No refactor. No change to `search.ts`, `academy/`, or `liturgy/`.

New tool `search_music_facts`: `minRole: 'member'`, `execution: 'server'`,
`confirm: false`. Server-side only, so no frontend or iOS deploy.

## Prompt integration

Two rules from main govern this, and the previous spec had both backwards.

1. **Never mention the library.** `prompt.ts:167`: "NEVER mention the library,
   its contents, or the fact that it lacked something... Just answer." The new
   tool follows this. No "my reference library doesn't cover that."
2. **`domainNote` needs a targeted edit, not an added block.** Today it says
   music theory gets "no search of any kind," which would forbid calling this
   tool. The edit is narrow, because this corpus is narrow: *concepts* still get
   answered directly from the model's own knowledge; only **specific numeric
   facts** — ranges, transpositions, clefs, score order, dates, personnel —
   route to `search_music_facts` first.

That narrowness is a feature. It preserves the rule's intent (a theory question
deserves an explanation, not a lookup) while covering the case the rule never
contemplated (a range question deserves a lookup, not a guess).

## Testing

- `corpus.test.ts` — every chunk has non-empty text; ids unique; every pitch
  parses as scientific pitch notation; every range has low < high. That last
  check is the one that catches transcription typos, which are the main risk in
  hand-curated data.
- `search.test.ts` — "viola range" → the viola chunk; "clarinet transposition"
  → the clarinet chunk; "B flat trumpet sounds" → trumpet; a query with no
  matching record returns zero hits.
- Vitest, not `deno test`. Gate on **added** failures — the repo baseline drifts
  (measured 2026-08-06 on `origin/main`: 4 failed files / 3 failed tests, 169
  typecheck errors).

## Out of scope

- Any prose from the source documents.
- The source `.md` files themselves. They are **not committed**; they are a
  reference Kevin reads while curating the data files.
- Embeddings. The lexical scorer is fine for a few hundred short records.
- Glossary definitions, answer cards, historical narrative.

## Decisions (resolved 2026-08-06)

Kevin: **"do all"** — full scope, practical ranges included.

1. **Practical ranges ship**, as GleeWorld's own guidance rather than reproduced
   opinion. They are the one field in this corpus that is judgment rather than
   physics, and they become a recommendation to ~50 choir directors, so they
   are a **review checkpoint before deploy**, not before build. Kevin sees the
   full list of practical ranges and signs off; anything he will not vouch for
   is dropped, and a chunk with a full range and no practical range is still
   useful.
2. **Full scope** — all six record types (~230), not the instruments-and-voices
   first pass.

## Extraction method

Records are **parsed, not retyped.** A script reads the three local `.md` files
and pulls only the structured factual fields — the `**Transposition:**` line,
the `**Range:**` bullets, the register table's Register and Span columns,
score-order lists, timeline years. Prose columns ("Character") and every
narrative paragraph are never read into the output.

This matters for accuracy as much as for copyright: 230 hand-typed pitch pairs
would carry transcription errors, and a wrong range is worse than no range.
The parse is mechanical and the tests assert every pitch is well-formed.

The source `.md` files stay **out of git** (they are the derived documents).
The extractor reads them from a local path; the committed artifacts are the
generated JSON facts and the corpus built from them. Re-running extraction
requires the local files; the JSON is the durable input.
