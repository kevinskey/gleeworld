# GleeWorld — Reading Music Module Expansion
### Full Music Theory Curriculum: Elementary → College

This expands the existing Reading Music module into a complete, leveled music theory curriculum. Four levels, each broken into units → lessons → exercises, with mastery gates between units. Everything is additive to the current module — no rewrites, the existing content becomes part of Level 1/2.

---

## Curriculum Architecture

```
Level (4) → Unit (6–10 per level) → Lesson (3–6 per unit) → Exercises (8–15 per lesson)
```

- **Mastery gate:** 80% on a unit quiz unlocks the next unit. Levels can be entered directly via a placement test.
- **Exercise engine types** (shared across all levels): multiple choice, audio identification, staff drag-and-drop (place a note/interval/chord), rhythm tap (tap-along grading), sight-singing (mic pitch detection — phase 2), keyboard identification, notation reading speed drills.
- **Placement test:** ~25 adaptive questions sampling all four levels; drops the student into the right unit.

## LEVEL 1 — ELEMENTARY (K–5)
*Goal: musical literacy foundations. Aligned with Kodály/Orff sequencing.*

### Unit 1.1 — Sound and Silence
- High vs. low, loud vs. soft, fast vs. slow
- Steady beat vs. rhythm
- Sound vs. silence (note vs. rest concept)

### Unit 1.2 — Rhythm Foundations
- Quarter note (ta), paired eighths (ti-ti), quarter rest
- Half note, whole note, half/whole rests
- Reading 4-beat rhythm patterns
- Rhythm tap exercises

### Unit 1.3 — The Staff
- Lines and spaces (5 lines, 4 spaces)
- Treble clef introduction
- Notes move up = sound goes up
- Line notes vs. space notes

### Unit 1.4 — Note Names (Treble)
- EGBDF / FACE
- Middle C and the C–C octave
- Letter name drills (timed identification)

### Unit 1.5 — Solfège & Melody
- Do–re–mi–sol–la (pentatonic core)
- Steps vs. skips vs. repeats
- Melodic contour (up/down/same)
- Echo singing patterns

### Unit 1.6 — Meter & Bar Lines
- Bar lines, measures, double bar
- 2/4, 3/4, 4/4 — counting beats per measure
- Time signature as "beats per box"

### Unit 1.7 — Musical Vocabulary
- Dynamics: piano, forte, crescendo, decrescendo
- Tempo: fast (allegro), slow (adagio) — concept level
- Repeat sign

**Level 1 Capstone:** read and clap an 8-measure rhythm; name 20 treble notes in 60 seconds; sing a pentatonic pattern from notation.

## LEVEL 2 — MIDDLE SCHOOL (6–8)
*Goal: full notation fluency in both clefs, basic tonal theory. Aligned with NAfME middle school standards and beginning choral/band method books.*

### Unit 2.1 — Grand Staff & Bass Clef
- Bass clef note names (GBDFA / ACEG)
- Grand staff, middle C as the bridge
- Ledger lines (2 above/below each staff)
- Octave identification (C4 = middle C)

### Unit 2.2 — Full Rhythm System
- Sixteenth notes and rests
- Dotted notes (dotted half, dotted quarter)
- Ties vs. slurs
- Eighth–sixteenth combinations (ti-tika, tika-ti)
- Pickup notes (anacrusis)

### Unit 2.3 — Accidentals & Half Steps
- Sharps, flats, naturals
- Half step vs. whole step
- Enharmonic equivalents (F# = Gb)
- The keyboard as a map

### Unit 2.4 — Major Scales & Key Signatures
- Major scale pattern (WWHWWHW)
- Key signatures to 4 sharps and 4 flats
- Order of sharps/flats
- Circle of fifths introduction
- "Last sharp +1 / second-to-last flat" identification tricks

### Unit 2.5 — Minor Scales (Natural)
- Relative major/minor relationship
- Natural minor scale
- Hearing major vs. minor (audio ID)

### Unit 2.6 — Intervals by Number
- Unison through octave (number only, no quality yet)
- Melodic vs. harmonic intervals
- Interval ear training: 2nds, 3rds, 5ths, octaves

### Unit 2.7 — Triads & Basic Harmony
- Major and minor triads (root position)
- Building triads on scale degrees
- I, IV, V — the primary chords
- Hearing chord changes in simple songs

### Unit 2.8 — Meter Deep Dive
- Simple meters: 2/4, 3/4, 4/4, cut time
- Compound meter introduction: 6/8
- Conducting patterns (2, 3, 4)

### Unit 2.9 — Expression & Score Reading
- Full dynamics range (pp–ff), sfz, accents
- Tempo terms: largo → presto, ritardando, accelerando, fermata
- Articulations: staccato, legato, marcato
- Navigation: D.C., D.S., coda, first/second endings

**Level 2 Capstone:** identify all key signatures to 4 accidentals; name notes in both clefs with ledger lines; clap dotted/sixteenth rhythms in simple and 6/8 meter; build major/minor triads.

## LEVEL 3 — HIGH SCHOOL (9–12)
*Goal: AP Music Theory readiness. A student finishing Level 3 should be prepared for the AP exam or a college theory placement test.*

### Unit 3.1 — Complete Key System
- All 15 major and minor key signatures
- Full circle of fifths fluency
- Parallel vs. relative keys

### Unit 3.2 — All Scale Forms
- Harmonic and melodic minor
- Chromatic scale
- Pentatonic (major/minor), whole tone, blues scale
- Modes introduction (Ionian–Locrian)

### Unit 3.3 — Intervals with Quality
- Major, minor, perfect, augmented, diminished
- Interval inversion
- Compound intervals
- Full interval ear training (all qualities within an octave)
- Tritone resolution

### Unit 3.4 — Triads & Seventh Chords
- All four triad qualities (M, m, dim, aug)
- Triad inversions and figured bass symbols (6, 6/4)
- Seventh chords: M7, Mm7 (dominant), m7, ø7, °7
- Seventh chord inversions (7, 6/5, 4/3, 4/2)
- Lead-sheet symbols vs. Roman numerals

### Unit 3.5 — Diatonic Harmony & Roman Numerals
- Roman numeral analysis in major and minor
- Chord function: tonic, predominant, dominant
- Harmonic progression norms (T–PD–D–T)
- Cadences: authentic (PAC/IAC), half, plagal, deceptive
- Cadence ear training

### Unit 3.6 — Voice Leading & Part Writing
- SATB ranges, spacing, doubling
- Motion types: parallel, similar, contrary, oblique
- Forbidden parallels (5ths/8ves)
- Tendency tones: leading tone and chordal 7th resolution
- Writing/error-detection exercises: realize a figured bass, harmonize a melody

### Unit 3.7 — Non-Chord Tones
- Passing tone, neighbor tone, suspension, retardation
- Appoggiatura, escape tone, anticipation, pedal point
- Identifying NCTs in score excerpts

### Unit 3.8 — Secondary Function & Modulation
- Secondary dominants (V/V, V/IV, etc.) and leading-tone chords
- Tonicization vs. modulation
- Modulation to closely related keys
- Pivot chord identification

### Unit 3.9 — Rhythm & Meter, Advanced
- Compound meters (6/8, 9/8, 12/8) fluency
- Asymmetric meters (5/4, 7/8)
- Triplets, duplets, syncopation, hemiola
- Two-part rhythm reading

### Unit 3.10 — Form & Analysis Basics
- Phrase, period, sentence structure
- Binary, ternary, rounded binary
- Verse-chorus and 12-bar blues
- Theme and variations

### Unit 3.11 — Aural Skills (runs parallel to 3.1–3.10)
- Melodic dictation (stepwise → leaps → chromatic)
- Harmonic dictation (bass line + Roman numerals)
- Sight-singing: major/minor, leaps within tonic/dominant triads, simple chromaticism
- Error detection (notation vs. performed audio)

**Level 3 Capstone:** AP-style exam — part writing, figured bass realization, melodic/harmonic dictation, Roman numeral analysis of a score excerpt, sight-singing assessment.

## LEVEL 4 — COLLEGE
*Goal: full undergraduate theory core (Theory I–IV equivalent) plus aural skills.*

### Unit 4.1 — Diatonic Harmony Review & Refinement
- Accelerated review of Level 3 with literature-based analysis
- 6/4 chord types (cadential, passing, pedal, arpeggiated)
- Sequences (descending fifths, 5–6, etc.)

### Unit 4.2 — Chromatic Harmony I
- Mode mixture / borrowed chords
- Neapolitan sixth
- Augmented sixth chords (Italian, French, German)
- Chromatic predominant function

### Unit 4.3 — Chromatic Harmony II
- Enharmonic modulation (°7 and Ger+6 reinterpretation)
- Common-tone diminished sevenths
- Chromatic mediants
- Modulation to distant keys
- Extended tertian harmony (9th, 11th, 13th chords)

### Unit 4.4 — Counterpoint
- Species counterpoint (1st–5th species, two voices)
- 18th-century style: invention and fugue technique
- Subject/answer, exposition, episode, stretto
- Canon types

### Unit 4.5 — Form & Analysis
- Sonata form (exposition, development, recapitulation)
- Rondo, sonata-rondo
- Concerto form, ritornello
- Fugue analysis
- Song forms and text-music relationships (art song, spirituals, hymnody)

### Unit 4.6 — Post-Tonal Theory
- Pitch-class sets, normal form, prime form, interval vectors
- Twelve-tone technique: rows, matrices, P/I/R/RI
- Centricity without functional tonality
- Modes of limited transposition, octatonic/hexatonic collections
- Minimalism, aleatory, spectral concepts (survey)

### Unit 4.7 — Jazz & Popular Harmony
- Lead sheet fluency, chord-scale theory
- Extensions and alterations (b9, #11, 13)
- ii–V–I and turnarounds, tritone substitution
- Modal interchange in pop/gospel
- Gospel harmonic vocabulary (passing diminished, "preacher chords," reharmonization)

### Unit 4.8 — Instrumentation & Score Skills
- Transposing instruments (Bb, Eb, F)
- Reading open score (SATB and orchestral)
- C clefs (alto, tenor)
- Ranges of common instruments and voices

### Unit 4.9 — Linear Analysis (Survey)
- Schenkerian concepts: Urlinie, prolongation, structural levels
- Reduction technique on short literature

### Unit 4.10 — Advanced Aural Skills
- Chromatic melodic dictation
- Four-part harmonic dictation
- Atonal interval/trichord identification
- Sight-singing: chromatic, modal, post-tonal melodies, complex meters

**Level 4 Capstone:** analyze a complete movement (Roman numeral + form), realize an advanced figured bass, complete a twelve-tone matrix exercise, transcribe a four-bar gospel/jazz progression by ear.

## Data Model (additive migration)

Spec table names use a generic shape; the real implementation adapts to the
GleeWorld schema: uuid PKs, `auth.users`-based `user_id uuid` (not
`gw_member bigint`), and multi-tenant `tenant_id uuid DEFAULT
current_tenant_id()` with RESTRICTIVE RLS on per-user tables. Curriculum
content tables (levels/units/lessons/exercises) are platform-global.

Implemented tables: `gw_theory_levels`, `gw_theory_units`,
`gw_theory_lessons`, `gw_theory_exercises`, `gw_theory_progress`,
`gw_theory_placements`. See
`supabase/migrations/20260611020000_theory_v2_phase1.sql`.

## Rendering & Audio Stack

- **Notation rendering:** VexFlow (client-side, free, renders from JSON) — no image assets needed for staff exercises.
- **Audio:** Tone.js for playback of intervals/chords/melodies generated from exercise JSON — no audio file library required for most ear training.
- **Rhythm tap:** capture tap timestamps in browser, grade against expected onsets with ±80ms tolerance (looser at Level 1, tighter at Level 4).
- **Sight-singing (Phase 4):** Web Audio pitch detection (e.g., pitchy/CREPE-lite) — flag as experimental.

---

## AI Layer (Anthropic API, server-side)

1. **Exercise generation:** endpoint that generates fresh exercises per lesson spec (e.g., "8 interval-ID questions, M/m 3rds and P5s, treble clef, keys to 2 sharps") → validated JSON → stored in `gw_theory_exercises`. Keeps drill content infinite without hand-authoring.
2. **Explain-my-mistake:** when a student misses a question, button calls the API with the question + their answer → one-paragraph kid-appropriate (level-aware) explanation.
3. **Placement summary:** after the placement test, generate a short narrative for the director: "Place in Level 3, Unit 3.4 — strong rhythm, weak interval quality."

## Build Phases (all behind `THEORY_V2` feature flag)

- **Phase 1:** migration + level/unit/lesson scaffolding + seed all four levels' unit/lesson structure from this spec + lesson reader UI. Existing Reading Music content maps into Level 1/2 lessons.
- **Phase 2:** exercise engine — multiple choice, staff placement, keyboard ID, speed drill + VexFlow rendering + progress tracking + mastery gates.
- **Phase 3:** audio — Tone.js ear training, rhythm tap, dictation + placement test.
- **Phase 4:** AI exercise generation + explain-my-mistake + sight-singing pitch detection (experimental).
